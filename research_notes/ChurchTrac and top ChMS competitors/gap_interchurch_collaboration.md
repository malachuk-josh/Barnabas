# Verification: Is a Consent-Based Collaboration Layer Between Independent Churches Really "Not Offered in the Marketplace"? (as of Nov 2026)

## Q1. Does any church management or church-adjacent product support opt-in data sharing or joint operations across separately owned churches?

### Takeaway
None of the church management systems checked (ChMS) lets separately owned churches share data or run joint work peer to peer. They offer three things instead: one church with many campuses (Planning Center, ChurchTrac, Rock, Realm, Tithely, Pushpay ChurchStaq); top-down visibility for a diocese or denomination over parishes it already governs (ParishStaq for Diocese, MinistryPlatform, Realm's denominational edition); or aggregate metrics only (Gloo Church Metrics Groups). Cross-church joint work does exist, but only in standalone, single-purpose tools outside the ChMS: CharityTracker, Meet the Need, CarePortal, Crisis Cleanup, JustServe, Serve Day and GriefShare's group finder.

### Cited Findings

Its FAQ says there is "no built-in option to merge organization account data." — [Planning Center FAQ](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/planning-center-faq.html); [Multiple accounts or multiple campuses](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/136812-multiple-accounts-or-multiple-campuses-.html) (search snippet; a second URL for that article returned 404)

- Inside one account, all subscribed Planning Center products share the same People data, and multi-campus features cover campus-specific folders, locations and groups. — [Planning Center FAQ](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/planning-center-faq.html) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Cross-church operations are split by use case. Benevolence goes to CharityTracker, Meet the Need or Wise Hope; foster and child-welfare needs to CarePortal; disaster work to Crisis Cleanup; volunteering to JustServe, Serve Day or ServiceReef; classes to curriculum publishers' finders. No one product spans all five examples, and none sits inside a ChMS.
- The diocese tools (ParishStaq for Diocese, MinistryPlatform) are the most capable multi-organization data models in church software. But their model is ownership plus role-based access, not consent between peers. A diocese sees parish data because it governs the parishes. That differs from independent churches choosing what to share.
- Gloo's assets (Church Metrics Groups, Explorer routing, Barna Cities) put it in the best position to build a city layer. Today, though, it shares only aggregates, and seeker routing is Gloo-controlled. Its privacy reputation may make consent-first positioning a real point of difference.
- Most ChMS vendors' per-church tenant architecture, shown by Planning Center's "cannot be merged/shared," makes a peer layer a structural change for them, not a quick feature add.

### Gaps
- Subsplash, Realm and Tithely help centers were not searched specifically for cross-organization features. The "none found" verdict for them rests on general searches and earlier competitor notes.
- ParishSOFT's diocese edition (Ministry Brands) was not read directly. Only a ParishSOFT blog title ("why dioceses choose parishsoft") and a Diocese of Richmond case study title ("centralized data and streamlined reporting") surfaced. — [ParishSOFT blog](https://www.parishsoft.com/blog/why-dioceses-choose-parishsoft); [Diocese of Richmond case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/parishsoft/case-study/the-diocese-of-richmond-customer-case-study)
- Southern Baptist association tools (Annual Church Profile systems, SBC association software) were not found in searchable form. Contacting a state convention would be needed.
- CityServe International's own platform and Love Your City organizers' tooling could not be confirmed. Search surfaced only events and the Mission One Eleven apps.
- Alpha's course finder and similar curriculum directories (Celebrate Recovery, Financial Peace) were not checked.
- Meet the Need's current number of cities, churches and users was not found. Its site gives no figures.

## Q2. Do benevolence-coordination tools exist (CharityTracker / Simon Solutions, Unite Us, findhelp, "Benevolence Hub")? How do they handle multiple churches sharing client records, and are they integrated with ChMS?

### Takeaway
Yes. Shared benevolence across churches is a mature, decades-old category, led by CharityTracker (Simon Solutions, since about 2007). CharityTracker already does the core "one person isn't helped twice" job: churches and agencies in a city join one network, and each note or assistance record can be shared with the network or kept private. It is a separate case-management system with no ChMS integration found. It has no volunteer, event or group features, and it is built around the client served, not the congregation. "Benevolence Hub" was not found as a product. Unite Us and findhelp-style social-care networks include faith groups as one partner type but are not church-designed.

### Cited Findings

**CharityTracker (Simon Solutions, Florence, Alabama)**
- Origin: created by Greg Hammond and Larry LaBarge of Simon Solutions. It lets benevolence organizations join networks, most of them regional or community-based, "to track people who move between groups asking for the same resources." The first network was in the Florence, AL area. Hammond: "Our heart at Simon Solutions is to help the body of Christ connect, to bring the body of Christ together." — [The Alabama Baptist, Nov 22 2007](https://thealabamabaptist.org/ccm-learns-about-charitytracker/)
- Current church landing page: "visibility you deserve on assistance provided by other churches," "connect and collaborate with other churches nearby or within your organization." It claims duplication reduced "by an average of 91% or more" (no method given). — [CharityTracker for Churches](https://churches.charitytracker.net/)
- Scale claims conflict: "thousands of churches in over 2,500 cities" [(churches page)](https://churches.charitytracker.net/); "more than 2,200 cities" [(church.charitytracker.net, search snippet)](https://church.charitytracker.net/); "over 462 cities" (an older figure on the sister product's page) [(Oasis Insight)](https://oasisinsight.net/why/).
- Pricing: "starting at just $20/month per user," with Basic $20, Plus $40 and Pro $60/month. — [CharityTracker for Churches](https://churches.charitytracker.net/). A pricing aggregator says the higher-volume plan includes "API integration," with no named targets. — [Spendbase vendor page (low-reliability aggregator)](https://test-partneway.prod.spendbase.co/vendors/charitytracker/)

There is no volunteer management, event registration or groups. — [CharityTracker for Churches](https://churches.charitytracker.net/)

### Inferences
- **On CharityTracker, the most important finding:** the "coordinated benevolence" use case is not white space. CharityTracker has done city-network benevolence sharing for churches and agencies since about 2007. It claims thousands of churches, per-record privacy controls and network admin roles, at low per-user prices. A new app pitching "so one person isn't helped twice" as novel would be inaccurate.
- What CharityTracker does not do, and where room remains:
  - It is a separate system with no named ChMS integration, so staff enter data twice.
  - It covers only the client being served. It has no volunteer pool, joint events, shared registration or cross-church groups.
  - Profiles are network-visible by default, and other agencies cannot make them private. The sources describe no explicit client-consent flow (see Gaps).
  - Data quality depends on manual search, so duplicates still occur.
  - It needs a lead agency to act as network administrator.
- Wise Hope and Love INC's homemade ACCESS tool show that local networks keep building their own tools. That suggests unmet need for better or more integrated tooling, though this is weak evidence.

### Gaps
- No CharityTracker help-center article describing client-consent or release-of-information handling was found. Whether clients sign a consent before their profile is shared network-wide is undocumented in the sources reached. Check the vendor or a network administrator directly.
- CharityTracker's API scope, and whether any church has connected it to a ChMS, could not be confirmed.
- findhelp (formerly Aunt Bertha) was not researched. Its faith-community programs and church use remain open.
- No independent study of how much CharityTracker reduces duplication was found. The 91% and 18% figures are vendor or secondary claims.

## Q3. For each near match, what exactly does it do, and how does it fall short of a true cross-church collaboration layer?

### Takeaway
Every near match covers one slice, sits outside the ChMS, or relies on a hierarchy rather than peer consent. The closest overall is Meet the Need: free, still live in 2026, and combining shared case management, volunteers and events across churches. Its scale and depth are undocumented, and it has no known ChMS integration. For benevolence alone, CharityTracker is a full, entrenched competitor.

### Inferences
- What no product found combines:
  - It lives inside or connected to the church's own ChMS, so people, volunteers and events need no double entry.
  - Each church chooses per data type what to share, peer to peer, with no governing body required.
  - It covers several joint-work types at once: benevolence, volunteers, events and registration, referrals, and cross-church groups.
  - It has explicit person-level consent for sharing member or volunteer data.
- This combination is the defensible white space. Any single use case on its own (benevolence de-duplication, a shared serve board, a disaster work-order map) already has an incumbent.
- Market risk: incumbents in each slice are free (CarePortal, Crisis Cleanup, JustServe, Meet the Need) or cheap (CharityTracker). A new entrant would likely need to integrate with them, for example by importing CarePortal alerts or syncing benevolence with CharityTracker, rather than replace them.

### Gaps
- No hands-on or demo-level confirmation of Meet the Need's current shared-case-management depth, permissions or user base.
- Whether CarePortal or CharityTracker has an API for third-party integration was not confirmed.

## Q4. What evidence shows that ministries want cross-church collaboration?

### Takeaway
Demand is well supported for joint serve days, city-movement networks, and benevolence coordination to stop repeat requests and fraud. That last need has kept CharityTracker, Wise Hope, Love INC and county programs alive for nearly 20 years. Hard, recent, quantified survey data on how many churches want shared software is thin. Most statistics are old (2006–2010) or come from interested parties (Gloo, vendors).

### Cited Findings

**Benevolence duplication and fraud**
- A pastor in the Wise Hope network: "Sixty to 70 percent of the people coming into our office were people coming in without real needs," and other pastors reported the same. A ministry chair said they sought a way to "curb abuses of the system." — [The Alabama Baptist, May 13 2010](https://thealabamabaptist.org/?p=55736)
- Wilson County, NC built church benevolence intake through the county to "minimize duplication of services and help reduce fraud." — [Wilson County DSS](https://www.wilsoncountync.gov/departments/social-services/services/faith-connections-406) (search snippet)
- A CharityTracker church customer cites savings from stopping "people abusing the system." — [CharityTracker for Churches](https://churches.charitytracker.net/)
- LifeWay Research (Feb 2009, 1,000 Protestant pastors): 62% of churches got more outside requests for financial help than in previous years, 31% saw more requests from within, and 37% raised spending on the needy. These figures are from the recession era. — [Christian Post, Mar 31 2009](https://www.christianpost.com/article/20090331/churches-adjust-to-challenging-times-do-more-good/)
- Scams hit multiple churches at once and churches warn each other informally. In Colorado, a sheriff warned "a coalition of churches," and at least eight churches reported similar incidents. — [CBS Colorado](https://cbsnews.com/colorado/news/scam-targets-churchgoers-jefferson-county-colorado); [Baptist Standard, 2008](https://baptiststandard.com/archives/2008-archives/scam-callers-preying-on-churches-compassion)

A product should probably plug into existing conveners (Love INC affiliates, city movements, associations, CarePortal affiliates) rather than expect churches to self-organize.
- The many homegrown efforts (Highlands' Serve Day app, Love INC Lufkin's ACCESS, county-run intake programs, Wise Hope) point to recurring unmet demand for tools that fit how churches already work.

## Q5. Verdict: does the gap claim hold, and are the four earlier findings confirmed?

### Takeaway
**The gap claim PARTLY HOLDS.**
- **Holds (narrow reading):** no church management system, and no single product, offers a consent-based, multi-purpose collaboration layer between separately owned churches that is tied to each church's own people, volunteer and event data.
- **Fails (broad reading):** "Churches can't share benevolence records or serve opportunities" is false. CharityTracker (benevolence, since about 2007, thousands of churches), Meet the Need (free, shared case management plus volunteers and events, still live in 2026), CarePortal, Crisis Cleanup and JustServe each already do part of the job across independent churches.
- **Messaging:** the team should not say cross-church benevolence coordination is "not offered." It should say the integrated, consent-first, multi-use-case layer is not offered.

### Cited Findings

**Earlier finding 1: "Gloo offers aggregated data for denominations and networks, but doesn't run the shared work itself."**
- **CONFIRMED.**
- Church Metrics Groups shares aggregate church-level metrics, invitation-only. Admins see the whole group; churches see only their own. — [Church Metrics Groups](https://support.churchmetrics.com/article/7466sry7bu-what-is-church-metrics-groups)
- Nuance: Gloo also runs cross-church seeker routing (Explorer, He Gets Us, Churches Care), so it does run one kind of shared operation, controlled by Gloo. — [RNS 2023](https://religionnews.com/2023/08/22/over-30-outreach-partners-have-joined-gloo-to-connect-more-people-to-local-ministries/)
- No collaboration features were found in Gloo+. — [Gloo help](https://help.gloo.us/en/articles/9156105-what-is-a-gloo-membership)

**Earlier finding 2: "Meet the Need is a small city-wide tool whose information is dated."**
- **PARTLY WRONG.**
- The site is maintained (copyright 2026; ebooks dated Feb 2025). It is free and lists shared case management, volunteer management and city-wide event management. That makes it the closest multi-purpose match, not a marginal one. — [meettheneed.org](https://meettheneed.org/)
- What is dated is the independent coverage (2011) and the lack of usage figures. — [Baptist News Global, 2011](https://baptistnews.com/article/computer-system-works-behind-the-scenes-to-help-churches-meet-needs/)

**Earlier finding 3: "State of Our City is a pilot project on community-needs data."**
- **UNVERIFIED.**
- No product or pilot named "State of Our City" was found. The closest matches are Barna and Gloo's "Barna Cities" (2021, five metros) and the State of the Church program with church-health dashboards. — [Columbus Dispatch via PressReader, 2021](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-columbus-dispatch/20210413/281496459105038); [Christian Post: Barna relaunches State of the Church](https://assets.christianpost.com/news/barna-relaunches-state-of-the-church-survey-in-a-personalized-version.html)
- The report writer should drop this name or replace it with "Barna Cities" unless the original source can be found.

**Earlier finding 4: "Mainstream church management systems stop at multiple campuses of a single church."**
- **CONFIRMED, with one refinement.**
- Planning Center explicitly cannot share or sync between accounts. — [Planning Center](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/136812-multiple-accounts-or-multiple-campuses-.html)
- Refinement: Catholic and denominational editions (ParishStaq for Diocese, MinistryPlatform, Realm's denominational edition) go beyond campuses to multi-parish or diocese rollups. That access is hierarchical, not opt-in among peers. — [Pushpay](https://pushpay.com/?p=14788); [ACST](https://www.acstechnologies.com/ministryplatform/catholic/)

**Omitted from the earlier findings:**
- CharityTracker is the most significant omission and a direct competitor on the benevolence use case. — [churches.charitytracker.net](https://churches.charitytracker.net/)
- Also missing from the earlier list: CarePortal, Crisis Cleanup, JustServe, Serve Day, Wise Hope (status unknown), Love INC clearinghouses and Unite Us networks (see Q1–Q2).

### Inferences
- **Honest positioning:** "the first ChMS-native, consent-based way for independent churches to do joint ministry: share volunteers, coordinate benevolence, run joint events and refer people, choosing exactly what to share."
- For benevolence specifically, interoperating with CharityTracker, or clearly beating it on ChMS integration and consent, is likely necessary. Many city networks are already on CharityTracker.

### Gaps
- Whether any 2026 startup has launched a ChMS-integrated inter-church layer could not be ruled out. Searches for new "church collaboration platform" startups returned only the older tools above.
- Pushpay, Planning Center, ACST and Gloo roadmaps (unannounced features) are unknown.
