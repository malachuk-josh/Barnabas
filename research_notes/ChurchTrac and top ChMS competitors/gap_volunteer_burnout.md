# Gap Verification: "Volunteer Burnout Radar" (cross-ministry burnout risk scoring + leader alerts)

Target feature being verified: a per-volunteer burnout or overload risk score that combines (a) total serving load across all teams and roles, (b) consecutive weeks served, (c) recent declined requests, and (d) drops in that person's own group or attendance engagement, then alerts a leader to step in. Research as of November 8, 2026. Searches ran across vendor sites, help centers, vendor blogs, and church research publications (about 31 search and fetch calls). Most evidence is vendor marketing copy. Each finding notes whether it is a documented product feature or general advice.

**Bottom-line verdict (for the report writer):** The strict claim "not currently offered in the marketplace" does **not** hold as worded. At least one shipping church product, **Church.IQ**, markets an "AI burnout detection (0–10 score)" weighted mainly by cross-department involvement and roster frequency. A second, **Vitals** (an analytics layer on Planning Center), markets "burnout risk" and "burnout detection" in its volunteer health dashboard. Neither one documents the full four-signal combination. Neither uses declined requests or a drop in the person's own group or attendance engagement as a burnout input, and neither documents proactive leader alerts. A defensible, narrower claim: *"No major ChMS (Planning Center, Pushpay/ChurchStaq, Tithely/Breeze, Rock, Realm, MinistryPlatform, Subsplash, ChurchTrac, Churchteams, ChMeetings) offers a burnout risk score. A few small, newer products offer simple load-based burnout scores. None that we found combines cross-ministry load, consecutive weeks, declines, and personal engagement drops into one score with leader alerts."*

## Q1. Does any church management, volunteer scheduling, or church engagement product offer burnout or overload risk scoring, cross-ministry over-scheduling alerts, or volunteer health dashboards?

### Takeaway
Yes, partly. Two small or newer church products market burnout scoring or detection: Church.IQ (a documented 0–10 weighted score) and Vitals (burnout detection with undisclosed inputs). None of the large ChMS vendors checked offers a burnout risk score. They offer rule-based building blocks instead: serving-frequency caps, inactivity reports, engagement or disengagement scores, and conflict alerts. The previous search missed Church.IQ, Vitals, Nurture's multi-signal at-risk alerts, and TouchPoint's engagement score.

### Inferences
- Church.IQ's score covers the core of the target concept: cross-department load is its heaviest weight (+4), and roster frequency (+3) partly stands in for consecutive weeks. Even so, a competitor can now truthfully say "burnout scoring exists." The team should not market its feature as a "world first" or claim burnout scoring is unavailable.
- Church.IQ's wording ("organisation," "roster") suggests a non-US, likely Australian or UK, origin. This is inferred from spelling only and not verified. Its size, customer count, and launch date are unknown. It looks like a small, recent entrant rather than an established ChMS.
- Vitals is the most direct partial competitor for a Planning Center–centric audience, since it already sits on PCO data and markets "burnout risk." Its inputs are undisclosed, so how much it overlaps can't be judged from public pages.
- The market splits into two camps that nobody we found has merged. Overload tools (PCO caps, GraceSquad, ServantFlow, Church.IQ) look at serving volume. Disengagement tools (Nurture, TouchPoint, Pushpay Insights, Churchteams 60-day report, Bloomerang lapsed reports) look at drops. The target feature's differentiator is combining the two: an overloaded volunteer who is *also* skipping their small group or declining more often.
- Declined requests as a burnout signal: no product found uses decline rate in a risk score, even though PCO and Rock store decline data natively.

### Gaps
- I couldn't find documentation for **MinistryPlatform, Elvanto, ChurchSuite, Faithlife Equip, ChurchAI, Servant Keeper, or Realm help-center pages** that addresses serving-load or burnout features. Targeted searches returned only general blogs. "Not found," not "confirmed absent."
- **Rock RMS** has engagement-style features (e.g., Streaks/Engagement types) according to prior knowledge. My search did not surface documentation, so I couldn't verify whether any church has built a burnout recipe on them.
- **Church.IQ's** company location, founding date, customer base, and whether the score triggers notifications are undocumented. A demo or trial would settle the alerting question.
- **Vitals'** burnout inputs, alerting, and pricing weren't visible on the pages fetched.
- **Nurture's** at-risk logic (signal weights, whether serving decline counts) isn't published.
- I did not do hands-on testing of any product. All findings come from public marketing or help pages.

### Inferences
- **What remains differentiated:** (1) using *declined requests* and *decline rate trend* as a burnout input; (2) counting *drops in the volunteer's own spiritual participation* (their small group or worship attendance falling while they keep serving) as a burnout signal rather than a disengagement signal; (3) *true cross-module load*: Services teams plus kids check-in rosters plus group leadership plus event volunteering, not just one scheduling module; (4) *proactive leader alerts with a suggested action* ("check in with Sarah; 9 straight weeks across 3 teams, skipped her group twice"); (5) explainability, showing which factors drove the score.
- **Wording recommendation for the report:** replace "not currently offered in the marketplace" with "not offered by any major ChMS; small entrants (Church.IQ, Vitals) offer basic load-based burnout scores; no product combines load, streaks, declines, and personal engagement drops with leader alerts." The evidence supports that wording.
- **Competitive risk:** Pushpay has the data pieces (Nurture multi-signal alerts, Staq Data Lake, "individual engagement summaries" on the AI roadmap) to add an overload dimension quickly. Pushpay's roadmap points directly at this space.

### Gaps
- No hands-on validation of how Church.IQ or Vitals compute scores, or whether they alert leaders.
- Whether Planning Center's "general" scheduling preference caps load across *all* Services teams, and whether it reaches other PCO apps (Check-Ins, Groups), wasn't confirmed from the help page text retrieved.

## Q4. What evidence shows volunteer burnout is a real ministry problem (shortages, "20% do 80%," post-COVID retention)?

### Takeaway
There is strong, multi-source evidence of volunteer shortages and post-COVID losses: Gallup, Lifeway, the Hartford Institute's EPIC study, and Evangelical Alliance UK. Lifeway names over-reliance on the same volunteers across ministries as a leading cause of burnout. The "20% do 80%" pattern is widely cited and directionally supported (Thumma & Bird), but its precise ratio is contested (Alban Institute). Several vendor-cited statistics are unsourced and should not be used.

### Cited Findings
**Volunteer decline and shortages**
- Gallup: 35% of Americans reported volunteering for a religious organization in 2021, down from 38% in 2020 and 44% in 2017 (cited by Christianity Today, Jan. 14, 2022). — [Christianity Today, Jan 2022](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2022/01/church-ministry-volunteer-gallup-survey-lifeway-wr)
- Lifeway Research (spring 2021): more than three-quarters of U.S. pastors were concerned about developing leaders and volunteers and about "people's apathy and lack of commitment"; more than two-thirds were concerned about training current leaders and volunteers. — [Christianity Today, Jan 2022](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2022/01/church-ministry-volunteer-gallup-survey-lifeway-wr)
- Evangelical Alliance UK (autumn 2021): 59% of church leaders saw volunteering decrease. 31% of members volunteered less during the pandemic. Some reopened churches hadn't resumed youth (25%) or children's (17%) ministry. — [Christianity Today, Jan 2022](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2022/01/church-ministry-volunteer-gallup-survey-lifeway-wr)
- Chuck Peters, Lifeway kids ministry director: "A lot of churches lost their long-term, reliable, go-to people and were left with no one." — [Christianity Today, Jan 2022](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2022/01/church-ministry-volunteer-gallup-survey-lifeway-wr)
- A later Lifeway figure: 77% of pastors say developing leaders and volunteers is an issue they need to address (search-result summary). — [Baptist Standard: Most churchgoers say they want to serve, but few do it](https://baptiststandard.com/news/faith-culture/most-churchgoers-say-they-want-to-serve-but-few-do-it)
- Lifeway poll of 1,000 Protestant pastors (2023): 42% of adult attendees have regular church responsibilities (greeting, teaching, child care), the same as 2008 and 2010 levels (search-result summary). — [Christian Post: Most churchgoers support volunteering, fewer actually serve](https://assets.christianpost.com/news/most-churchgoers-support-volunteering-fewer-actually-serve.html)
- Lifeway survey of 1,002 Protestant churchgoers (Sept. 19–29, 2022; ±3.3%): 30% said they volunteered (through a group, for community service) in the past year (search-result summary). — [Baptist Standard](https://baptiststandard.com/news/faith-culture/most-churchgoers-say-they-want-to-serve-but-few-do-it)
- Hartford Institute EPIC study: volunteerism fell from about 40% of membership in early 2020 to about 20% by March 2022. By 2023, majority-white and multiracial congregations were at about 35% (vs. near 40% before the pandemic), and Black-majority congregations were back to about 30%, their pre-pandemic level. EPIC samples ranged from 140 to 2,802 congregations per group, ±~4 pts. *Caveat: these figures come from search-result summaries of SSRC/RNS coverage; the primary pages returned 403 or unreadable PDF. Different waves measure different things.* — [SSRC Intersections / RNS, Jan 2024](https://intersections.ssrc.org/2024/01/11/pandemic-boosted-resilience-hurt-financial-health-of-black-churches-says-report/?pdf=1855); [ChurchLeaders reprint](https://churchleaders.com/news/465506-pandemic-boosted-resilience-rns.html/2); [RNS, Sept 2023](https://religionnews.com/2023/09/05/giving-is-up-at-churches-and-people-are-back-but-clergy-are-thinking-about-quitting/)

**Over-reliance and burnout from load (directly supports the cross-ministry load signal)**
- Lifeway Research ("5 signs of burnout in your volunteers"): a frequent problem is relying too much on specific volunteers, "so much so that the volunteers burn out and quit serving." Leaders are advised to check whether the same people volunteer in all of their ministry areas, and to watch for "seasons when they missed a few weeks" and conversations expressing weariness (search-result summary). — [Lifeway Research, Apr 2019](https://lifewayresearch.com/2019/04/03/5-signs-of-burnout-in-your-volunteers/)
- Churchteams offers a counterpoint: "Volunteer burnout usually isn't caused by too much serving. It's often caused by feeling unseen." This supports including care and recognition signals, not just load. — [Churchteams](https://go.churchteams.com/preventing-church-volunteer-burnout/)
- ACST (Realm vendor) frames lack of visibility as the core problem: "No one can easily say who serves, how often, or where the gaps are … so leaders plan from memory." — [ACST](https://www.acstechnologies.com/church-growth/church-volunteer-management-software/)
- Data fragmentation: only 1 in 4 Pushpay churches use Check-In, Volunteer Schedules, and Process Queues together (search-result summary of Pushpay blog). — [Pushpay Staq Transformation](https://pushpay.com/blog/what-to-expect-from-pushpays-staq-transformation/)

**"20% do 80%"**

### Inferences
- The need is well documented. Volunteer pools shrank after COVID and haven't fully recovered in many congregations (EPIC 2023: ~35% vs ~40% pre-pandemic for white/multiracial congregations). Pastors widely name volunteer development as a top concern, and Lifeway explicitly points to the "same people in all ministry areas" pattern as a burnout cause. That pattern is exactly the cross-ministry load signal the feature targets.
- The Churchteams "feeling unseen" framing and Volunteer Circle's care-recency product suggest the radar should combine load signals with appreciation and check-in recency, so it does more than flag overload.
- Rising pastor AI adoption (45–61% depending on survey) makes a predictive or AI-flavored feature more acceptable to buyers now than a few years ago.
