# Competitor Profiles: Realm (ACS Technologies), Rock RMS (Spark Development Network), MinistryPlatform (ACS Technologies)

Research date: November 8, 2026. US market focus. Depth: Realm and Rock RMS in depth, MinistryPlatform at moderate depth.

Source caveats for the report writer:
- Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp blocked direct fetching (HTTP 403). Their ratings and review counts below come from search-engine snippets of those pages, not from fetching them. Treat the exact numbers as approximate snapshots from November 2026.
- Many 2026 "review" pages (costbench.com, churchmemberpro.com, chms.org, learnofchrist.com, softwareconnect.com and others) are affiliate or SEO comparison blogs, and some sell competing products. Their figures are marked as third-party throughout.
- The Rock community blog and release-notes pages need a login for full article text. Some Rock v18/v19 details therefore come from Rock's official X posts, blog headlines and search snippets of the release notes.

## 1. Company background: ownership, HQ, claimed churches served, hosting model

### Takeaway
Realm and MinistryPlatform now belong to the same company. Vanco (a Great Hill Partners portfolio company) acquired ACS Technologies in December 2025, and the combined faith business runs under the ACS Technologies (ACST) name, HQ Florence, South Carolina, claiming about 40,000 to 50,000 churches and organizations. MinistryPlatform joined ACS in 2021, when ACS merged in Think Ministry. Rock RMS is free, open-source software from Spark Development Network, an Arizona 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Churches self-host it (Windows/IIS/SQL Server) or buy managed hosting from partners such as Rock Cloud (Triumph Tech).

Vanco's faith organization "will operate under the ACS Technologies name," and Vanco's payments capabilities "will be woven into ACST's leading platforms, including Realm and MinistryPlatform." ### Inferences
- ChurchTrac now faces one consolidated ACST/Vanco competitor with two ChMS products (Realm for small to mid-size churches, MinistryPlatform for large and Catholic or diocesan organizations), plus in-house payments from Vanco. A bundled ChMS-plus-payments pitch is likely, and so is pressure to move to Vanco processing.
- ACST is now owned by private equity (through Vanco/Great Hill). That often brings price increases and product consolidation, but nothing confirms ACST plans to retire or merge Realm or MinistryPlatform.
- Rock's lack of a public adoption count is notable. The 2017 numbers suggest a deep but relatively small install base, concentrated in large churches.

### Gaps
- No current church counts for Realm alone, MinistryPlatform alone, or Rock (Rock's rockrms.com/organizations directory was not fetched).
- The Vanco/ACST deal value and any product-roadmap commitments (for example, whether Realm Giving moves to Vanco processing) were not found.
- Life.Church's current use of Rock as its primary ChMS is unverified; only third-party sources assert it.
- Spark's exact HQ city was not confirmed from a primary source. The organization is in Arizona, and RX26 is in Phoenix (see section 6).

## 2. Feature coverage by module

### Takeaway
All three are full-suite ChMS platforms. Realm stands out for native, integrated church fund accounting with payroll, AP and a segment-based chart of accounts. Rock stands out for its workflow and automation engine, Lava templating, built-in CMS, full source and SQL access, and plugin ecosystem. MinistryPlatform stands out as a deeply customizable database with routines, views and notifications, the PocketPlatform app and, since 2026, a natural-language AI and MCP layer. Worship planning is a weak spot for both Realm and Rock (commonly paired with Planning Center Services).

### Cited Findings

**Realm (ACS Technologies)**
- **Modules listed on the official site:** Dashboards, Reporting, Pathways, Communications (with a Constant Contact partner link), Groups, Mobile Apps ("Connect" for congregants and "Shepherd" for leaders), Events, Check-In, Volunteer Management, Background Checks, Giving, Contributions, Accounting (with Payroll), Profiles & Directories, Security, Websites and Integrations. The site also has solution pages for Pastoral Care, Administration, Children's & Youth, Worship Planning, Stewardship, Catholic, Denominational & Regional Offices, and Missions & Outreach. — [ACS Realm page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/)
- **Positioning quote:** "We intentionally built Realm to serve the needs of administrators." Realm Connect lets congregants "stay connected with your church and small groups, give online, update info." — [ACS Realm page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/)
- **Feature listings:** pledge management, child check-in, built-in accounting, member portal, multi-site management, attendance tracking (vendor-style listing). — [GetApp Realm listing (search snippet)](https://www.getapp.com/nonprofit-software/a/realm/)
- **Worship planning (conflict):** a third-party review says Realm does not cover service building, song management or musician scheduling and recommends pairing it with Planning Center Services. — [chms.org Realm review 2026 (third-party)](https://chms.org/support/realm-review-2026). ACS's site does list a "Worship Planning" ministry page, so how deep this feature goes is unclear. — [ACS Realm page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/)
- **Realm Accounting, detailed from the official brief:** — [Realm Accounting Brief (PDF, Feb 2025)](https://acstechnologies.com/realm/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2025/02/Realm-Accounting-Brief.pdf)
  - "Fully integrated into your Realm Contributions": contribution batches post as deposits to the General Ledger, which removes duplicate entry.
  - True fund accounting: churches create multiple funds and use them when entering transactions, which shows how money is spent.
  - Supports both **cash and accrual** accounting methods.
  - **Segment-based chart of accounts:** "Accounting segments allow finance staff flexibility to use any combination of funds, core accounts, and department segments when entering transactions."
  - **Accounts payable:** vendors and payees, recurring invoices, check generation and check runs.
  - **Budgets:** create, import and export.
  - **Audit trail:** a "detailed change log that records information such as posted transactions, edited and deleted budgets, and canceled check runs."
  - **Reports:** statements, general ledger, accounts payable and more.
  - **Payroll:** "Manage staff payroll within Realm Accounting."
  - **Permissions:** access limited to "only those who need it."
  - **Other:** project tracking, recurring transactions, detailed account inquiries; church-specific terminology; cloud-based.
- ACS describes "true fund accounting," where churches build the general ledger around their funds, plus deposits, disbursements, bank reconciliation, vendors, budgets and financial reports. — [ACS Accounting solutions page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/solutions/accounting)
- A third-party review rates Realm stronger than competitors on accounting integration and group management, because giving data "flows directly into their accounting module." — [chms.org Realm review 2026 (third-party)](https://chms.org/support/realm-review-2026)
- Payroll was flagged in ACS's July 2026 navigation as part of Accounting ("Accounting (with Payroll)"). — [ACS Realm page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/)

## 3. Pricing (date-stamped November 2026)

### Takeaway
Realm publishes only its entry tier: Inform, $49/month after a 30-day free trial (official, November 2026). Connect and Multiply are quote-based; third parties estimate about $44–47/month and about $199–209/month plus setup fees, and accounting is an add-on. Rock's software is free, but real cost is hosting plus people: managed Rock Cloud runs $419–$4,419/month by attendance (plus a $249 setup fee), and self-hosting on Azure runs about $50–500+/month plus staff or partner implementation. MinistryPlatform is quote-only; unverified aggregator listings say "starting at $500/month."

## 5. Target church size, positioning, and why churches choose or leave

### Takeaway
Realm targets administrator-led small to mid-size and larger churches (including Catholic and denominational offices) that want an all-in-one cloud system with real fund accounting. Rock targets medium to large, often multi-site churches (generally 500+ attendees) with in-house tech staff or a partner, who want ownership, automation and customization. MinistryPlatform targets large churches (1,000+ members), Catholic parishes, dioceses and denominational offices that need a configurable enterprise database.

### Cited Findings

**Realm**
- "We intentionally built Realm to serve the needs of administrators." There are solution pages for Catholic and for Denominational & Regional Offices. — [ACS Realm page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/)
- The tiered plans (Inform, Connect, Multiply) and the low entry price ($49) are aimed at smaller churches, while Multiply serves larger ones. — [ACST store](https://buy.acst.com/realm-inform/); [ACS Realm page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/)
- **Why chosen:** accounting integration and group management. — [chms.org (third-party)](https://chms.org/support/realm-review-2026). Ease of use, giving, directory and communications. — [Capterra (snippet)](https://www.capterra.com/p/146265/Realm/reviews/?page=3)
- **Why left or frustrated:** support quality, online-giving problems (2024), lost accounting flexibility vs. legacy ACS, and no worship planning (pairing with Planning Center Services). — [Capterra (snippets)](https://www.capterra.com/p/146265/Realm/reviews/?page=8); [chms.org](https://chms.org/support/realm-review-2026)

**Rock RMS**
- Best for medium-to-large churches (500+ members or weekly attendees) with technical staff or a Rock partner. It is "generally not recommended for churches under 300 members without technical resources." Best for "tech-savvy large churches wanting full control." — [ChurchMemberPro (third-party)](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/rock-rms-review/)
- Rock is "most popular with medium-to-large congregations." — [search summary of third-party review](https://softwarereview.com/s/rock-rms)
- Rock Cloud's smallest plan covers up to 1,000 attendees, consistent with a mid-to-large target. — [Rock Cloud pricing](https://www.rockcloud.com/pricing)
- **Why chosen:** no license fees, workflow automation, source access, data ownership and portability, community, multi-site, built-in CMS. — [ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/rock-rms-review/); [rockrms.com/hosting](https://www.rockrms.com/hosting)
- **Why avoided or left:** technical burden (IIS, SQL Server, .NET updates), implementation time (2–6 months), hidden staff and hosting costs, and a UI that overwhelms non-technical staff. — [LearnOfChrist](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/rock-rms); [ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/rock-rms-review/)

**MinistryPlatform**
- Headline: "Ministry Management Software for Large Churches," also targeting parishes, dioceses and denominational offices. The page leads with a Catholic-focused section. — [ACST MinistryPlatform page](https://www.acstechnologies.com/ministryplatform/)
- "Designed for large churches looking for the full scope of Church Management features." — [Capterra MP (snippet)](https://capterra.com/p/110437/MinistryPlatform/). "Designed for churches with 1,000 or more active members." — [Charity Charge](https://www.charitycharge.com/nonprofit-resources/church-management-software/)
- **Why chosen:** customizability, a single database across parishes and operations, and role-based dashboards. **Why frustrated:** learning curve, IT dependence, long implementation. — [Capterra MP (snippet)](https://capterra.com/p/110437/MinistryPlatform/reviews/); [Fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/ministryplatform)

### Inferences
- Positioning summary versus ChurchTrac, which serves small churches at low cost (per the task framing):
  - **Realm** is the closest competitor in the mid-market. It overlaps ChurchTrac's accounting appeal, but with deeper GL, AP and payroll, and with ACS/Vanco scale.
  - **Rock** and **MinistryPlatform** are upmarket. They are not direct competitors for small churches, but they set the bar for automation, AI and extensibility that larger prospects expect.
- A ministry-leader-focused app (not admin-focused) is underserved by all three. Realm is explicitly built for administrators, and Rock and MinistryPlatform need technical staff to configure good leader experiences. Realm's "Shepherd" leader app is the most direct overlap and should be examined further.

### Gaps
- No primary-source data on churn or switching flows (for example, Realm to Planning Center, or Rock to MinistryPlatform).
- Realm's "Shepherd" leader app capabilities were not documented in fetched sources.

### Inferences
- The AI race in large-church ChMS is now concrete.
